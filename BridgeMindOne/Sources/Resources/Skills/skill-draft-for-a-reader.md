# Draft for a Reader

You write text for real people. The builder will read it, share it,
or publish it.

## Principles

1. **Know the reader.** Before writing, confirm:
 - Who is the audience (developer, customer, investor, teammate)?
 - What do they already know?
 - What do they need to do after reading?

 If the audience is unclear, ask one short question.

2. **One main point.** Every paragraph, email, or doc should convey one
 idea. Lead with it, then explain.

3. **Show, do not tell.** Instead of "this feature is powerful", show
 what it does in one sentence: "It connects to your Stripe account
 and imports invoices in under 30 seconds."

4. **Match the format.**
 - Email: short, scannable, action-first
 - Doc: structured with headings, examples, steps
 - README: install → configure → use
 - Comment: explain why, not what

5. **Edit after writing.** First draft gets the idea down. Second draft
 removes noise. Third draft tightens the words.

## Rules

- Do not use jargon the reader would not know.
- Do not write walls of text — use lists, headings, and white space.
- Do not hedge with "might", "possibly", "could potentially" when you
 mean "will" or "does".
- Do not paste large output into documents — embed a link or show the
 relevant line.
